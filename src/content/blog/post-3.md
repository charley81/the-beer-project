Welcome to the heart of the Hops & Harvest technical series. Attenuation—the percentage of sugars converted into alcohol and CO2 by yeast—is the metric that defines your beer's body and finish.

A beer like our cellared Stout vertical (Image #8) needs profound attenuation to achieve its silky texture without cloying sweetness.

### Why Simple Temperature Targets Fail

When you are managing fermentation (especially high-gravity worts), you cannot just set your glycol-jacketed fermenter (Image #3) to "68°F" and walk away. Attenuation is not linear.

The yeast’s ability to convert complex sugars (maltotriose) shifts dramatically as the alcohol concentration increases (gravity drops) and the initial nutrient supply diminishes.

### The Attenuation Curve: Temp vs. Gravity

This is the optimization zone. You must manage the interaction of _Fermentation Temperature_ and _Specific Gravity_ (SG) over time.

| Phase                         | Time (Days) | Target Temp (°F)     | Target Gravity (SG) | Yeast Management Objective                                                                                                                                                                            |
| :---------------------------- | :---------- | :------------------- | :------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **LAG PHASE**                 | 0-1         | 64°                  | Max                 | Establish healthy, viable cell count. (Nutrient addition needed here).                                                                                                                                |
| **LOG PHASE (ACTIVE)**        | **2-5**     | **68°**              | **Fast Drop**       | **Max Vigor.** Manage the temperature rise caused by active fermentation. Hold steady.                                                                                                                |
| **STATIONARY PHASE (LATE)**   | **6-9**     | **72°**              | **Slow Drop**       | **Cleanup.** Allow the temperature to rise _slowly_. This forces the yeast to stay active and consume diacetyl, acetaldehyde, and other off-flavors (we identify these in our Sensory Kit, Image #7). |
| **TERMINAL GRAVITY (FINISH)** | **10-14**   | **34° (Cold Crash)** | **Terminal**        | **Maximum Attenuation.** Once SG is stable for 3 days, cold-crash to drop the yeast out (we need clarity before packaging!).                                                                          |

### Conclusion: Trust Your Data QA (Image #16)

Do not leave attenuation to chance. If you fail to manage that late 72°F rise, your yeast will go dormant too early, resulting in a under-attenuated beer that is sweet, thick, and prone to unstable bottle conditioning.

For complex, high-quality finishes (the kind we aim for with the cellared Stout vertical, Image #8), precise temperature management during the entire curve is non-negotiable.
